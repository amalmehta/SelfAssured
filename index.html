      How the scores are worked out

      Each part score is the average of your answers in that part, stretched onto 0–100:

      S= x‾−xmin xmax−xmin ×100
      S
      the part score, 0 to 100 (no units)
      x‾
      your average answer in that part, after flipping reversed statements
      xmin,xmax
      the lowest and highest answer values: 0 and 4 for Rosenberg, 1 and 5 for IPIP

      In plain words: 0 means you chose the least confident answer every time, 100 means the most confident every time, and 50 is the middle of the scale. Fifty is not the average person.

      Flipped statements. Some statements point the other way, like “I certainly feel useless at times.” Strongly agreeing with one of those counts as the least confident answer. Mixing directions stops people scoring high just by agreeing with everything.

      Steadiness is 100 minus the IPIP Vulnerability score, so that higher always means more self-assured. Overall is the plain average of the four parts. That average is our choice, not a published measure.

      Estimate The three short parts have only 4 questions each, so small differences are noise. Treat gaps of under about 10 points as “about the same”. That 10 is our rough guide, not a figure from the papers.
